\section*{Appendix B. Further Statistics}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix B. Further Statistics}

\noindent\textbf{Method details.} A substitution between a partial and a full date costs 0.5 in RDS, a rule whose effect we confirmed is negligible. The length floor is $|n-m|/\max(n,m)$, and RDS equals floor plus $(1-\text{floor})$ times a floor-adjusted score. On the 2,250-question subset the 29.5-point gap has interval [28.1, 30.9], the reasoning part 23.5 [22.1, 24.9], the question part 6.0 [5.0, 7.1], and the reasoning share 79.7\% [76.4, 82.9].

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RQ1.} The time duration gain gap (English gain minus Hindi gain), +2.3 pooled, is $-$5.2 with no distractor, +14.5 with a time-related one and $-$2.2 with an unrelated one; only +14.5 is significant. Hindi CoT's harm at the short level ($-$4.3 pooled) comes from the distractor conditions ($-$1.9 [$-$4.5, 0.7] without one, $-$6.2 and $-$4.7 with). Both Hindi task losses in RQ1 stay significantly negative with either distractor. Under strict scoring the gap under CoT grows from 23.0 to 31.1; the logistic trend has OR 0.97 per level.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RQ2.} The Hindi direct-to-CoT penalty for time-related sentences has interval [0.6, 3.8]. Comparing languages, the Hindi-minus-English penalty is +2.6 [0.4, 4.7] answered but +1.4 [$-$0.7, 3.5] strict, as English CoT's drop grows from 0.9 to 2.2 [1.3, 3.2]; it is not significant on logit specifications unless recurrence is removed. With unrelated sentences it is +2.6 [0.5, 4.6] answered and +2.8 [0.8, 4.8] strict, but because English CoT resists a fall in English direct answers: the Hindi penalty itself is null (+0.7). Of the 92 cut-off traces under the January sentence, none is a verbatim loop, and in a systematic sample of 23, read from excerpts by one LLM annotator, 18 mainly search for a January start date. Removing that sentence takes the Hindi recurrence penalty from +4.5 to +0.3 [$-$4.6, 4.9]; English stays at +8.5 (strict +9.9). For mentions, the English OR on the stricter list is 0.57 [0.38, 0.85] (0.92 on the original list); unrelated sentences give 1.15 and 1.30.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RQ3.} The reasoning part is 22.0 to 24.5 points in each distractor condition, and the two parts are not distinguishable at the short level. The direct-answer gap is 4.5 [3.3, 5.8]. Verification is associated with 63.6\% [39.8, 97.0] of en-CoT's day-of-week residual to English. CLP gains +9.0, +10.4 and +13.3 over Hindi CoT by distractor condition, and beats Hindi direct by 15.1 [13.5, 16.7], but is significantly worse on interval date (35.3\% vs 46.8\%). en-CoT beats CLP by 12.6 [11.4, 13.9]; on MGSM \citeauthor{qin-etal-2023-cross} report CLP 70.6 and En-CoT 57.8. Within the same question, the stage-1 split differs by +62.5 points [57.1, 68.0], against about 7 for the same split on en-CoT; \citeauthor{qin-etal-2023-cross} also note that stage 1 may give preliminary answers. For Hinglish, date duration falls by 5.5 points and date subtraction rises by 5.1 on Hindi questions; 25.5\% of Hindi-question traces have no Devanagari. Traces with Hindi function words lose 11.3 points more [9.0, 13.6] within task, level and distractor (8.4 to 12.0 across 48 threshold pairs). On Hindi questions Roman traces do no better than Devanagari ones ($-$2.8 [$-$5.2, $-$0.5]; $-$2.3 [$-$5.0, 0.4] within question). For recurrence, non-standard ordinals at $k=2$ to 4 give backward answers on 2 of 229 en-CoT questions, and removing $k=1$ moves en-CoT's gap to English only from 6.0 to 5.3. en-CoT's gap to English narrows from 10.2 points at short to 4.3 at very very long, but without $k\le 2$ the change is +0.8 [$-$2.5, 4.1].

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RQ4.} The answer-swap check uses the 11,309 both-correct thinking-off pairs and raises mean RDS by 0.082, against a 0.120 gap to disagreeing pairs. With op types including answer ops the OR per SD is 1.21. The fall in OR per 0.1 appears in each distractor condition (5.37, 4.60, 4.95 to 1.13, 1.08, 1.09). Within a question, op-type divergence barely separates disagreeing from agreeing variants (concordance 0.516 [0.501, 0.530]), and a within-question slope survives Holm correction only for date recurrence (OR per 0.1, 1.42 [1.25, 1.61]) and day of week (1.26 [1.11, 1.43]). Op types separate a different question from the same question by only +0.012. The floor is 58--61\% of mean RDS on standard content and on thinking-mode reasoning. Raw RDS does not separate the two on matched questions (+0.005 [$-$0.002, 0.011]), but the floor-adjusted score does (+0.088 [0.072, 0.105]). Counting unanswered as wrong on the same 450 questions, disagreement is 10.9\% with thinking on against 31.7\% off (OR 0.23 [0.18, 0.30]), with thinking and budget changing together.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RQ5.} Agreement with the API on 90 calibration questions is $-$2.2 points [$-$13.3, 7.8], which does not establish that the two are the same; the Hindi difference on 278 questions is [0.5, 10.8]. Deletion with sampling gives +4.5 [$-$2.7, 11.7]. The net 8.8\% has interval [5.5, 12.1], and resampling the 29 sentences widens the Q$-$P interval to [$-$0.3, 9.4]. The mention test is (M$-$P)$-$(P$-$P1) $=-$4.6 [$-$9.6, 0.6], with P$-$P1 $-$0.7 [$-$3.5, 2.1]. In later-mention traces, rollouts with no mention match the no-distractor arm (+0.0 [$-$4.8, 4.7]) and those with a mention do not (+8.7 [3.7, 13.6]); these subgroups are post-treatment. On the API, Hindi non-completion barely moves with the distractor (0.81\% to 0.89\%).
