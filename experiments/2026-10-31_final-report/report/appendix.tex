\section*{Appendix A. Worked Examples}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix A. Worked Examples}

\noindent\textbf{Hinglish, English question, wrong.} Date subtraction, short. ``Today is 2025-05-01, what was the date 3 days ago?'' Gold 2025-04-28. Excerpt: \emph{01 minus 3 karne par hum 31 April par aayenge} (`subtracting 3 from 01 brings us to 31 April'). Answer: 2025-04-30.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{en-CoT, backward answer.} Date recurrence, short, ID 5, on \hi{आज को छोड़कर, 1वीं आवृत्ति} (\emph{āj ko choṛkar, 1vīṃ āvṛtti}, `excluding today, the 1st occurrence'). Excerpt: \emph{Given the phrasing ``excluding today,'' this suggests we need to find what comes before.} Answer: 2025-04-06. Gold 2025-04-14.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{CLP, stage 1 anchors stage 2.} Date recurrence, long, ID 12. Stage 1 ends: \emph{the date of the 7th occurrence is 2 August 2027.} Stage 2 ends: \emph{Adding 78 days to 16 May 2027 also results in 2 August 2027.} Gold 2027-08-15.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Hindi CoT subtracts by walking forward.} Date subtraction, very very long, ID 79. Today is 2027-05-09; what was the date 52 days ago? Gold 2027-03-18. Excerpt, translated: \emph{From 9 May to 31 May is 22 days. \dots\ April has 30 days, so the date exactly 30 days before 30 April is 1 May.} Answer: 2027-05-01, after today.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Hindi CoT returns a ``week'' of two days.} Interval date, long, ID 95. Today is 2030-05-15. Gold 2030-05-20 to 2030-05-26. Excerpt, translated: \emph{15 May 2030 is a Saturday. \dots\ The next Monday will be 18 May 2030. \dots\ Sunday will be 19 May 2030 (6 days after Monday).} 15 May 2030 is a Wednesday.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{English CoT is one day late.} Day of week, very very long, ID 20, 2029-07-02. Gold Monday. Excerpt: \emph{Total: 31 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 2 = 183 days.} Answer: Tuesday. 1 January to 2 July is 182 days.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{English CoT skips 29 February.} Date recurrence, medium, ID 60. Today is 2028-02-15, every 6 days, 4th occurrence. Gold 2028-03-10. Excerpt: \emph{From 2028-02-27 to 2028-03-05: 28 - 27 = 1 day in February, plus 5 days in March = 6 days \ok} Answer: 2028-03-11. That gap is 7 days, and the check repeats the mistake.

\section*{Appendix B. Further Statistics}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix B. Further Statistics}

\noindent\textbf{Standard set.} The Hindi direct-to-CoT penalty for time-related sentences has interval [0.6, 3.8], and removing any one sentence leaves it between 1.8 and 2.6. The Hindi-minus-English penalty is +2.6 [0.4, 4.7] answered but +1.4 [$-$0.7, 3.5] strict. Removing the January sentence takes the Hindi recurrence penalty from +4.5 to +0.3 [$-$4.6, 4.9]; English stays at +8.5. Of the 92 cut-off traces under that sentence, none is a verbatim loop. Under strict scoring the CoT gap grows from 23.0 to 31.1 with difficulty.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{RDS.} Of 21,600 loaded pairs, 938 (4.3\%) are dropped because an answer could not be parsed, leaving 20,662 on 6,739 questions. With thinking off, 11,309 pairs are both correct, 5,947 English only, 505 Hindi only and 1,710 both wrong. Disagreement rises from 26.7\% on short questions to 38.6\% on very very long ones. A substitution between a partial and a full date costs 0.5 in RDS. These are in-sample associations.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{en-CoT and CLP.} The 29.5-point gap has interval [28.1, 30.9], the reasoning part 23.5 [22.1, 24.9], the question part 6.0 [5.0, 7.1], and the reasoning share 79.7\% [76.4, 82.9]. The two parts are not distinguishable at the short level. CLP gains +9.0, +10.4 and +13.2 over Hindi CoT by distractor condition. en-CoT beats CLP by 12.6 [11.4, 13.9]; on MGSM \citeauthor{qin-etal-2023-cross} report CLP 70.6 and En-CoT 57.8.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Hinglish.} On Hindi questions, Hinglish lowers date duration by 5.5 points and raises date subtraction by 5.1, which cancel in the mean. Roman traces do no better than Devanagari ones ($-$2.8 [$-$5.2, $-$0.5]).

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Interventions.} The net 8.8\% has interval [5.5, 12.1], and resampling the 29 sentences widens the Q$-$P interval to [$-$0.3, 9.4]. The mention test is (M$-$P)$-$(P$-$P1) $=-$4.6 [$-$9.6, 0.6]. Agreement with the API on 90 calibration questions is $-$2.2 points [$-$13.3, 7.8], which does not establish that the two are the same.
