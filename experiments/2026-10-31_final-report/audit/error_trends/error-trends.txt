A script labels every wrong answer with three things:

     how far off it is, in days, minutes or weekdays;
     the kind of mistake: off by one, wrong direction, whole hours off, 12-hour clock, cut off,
     and so on;
     what the question involves: crossing midnight, a month end or Feb 29.

Comparisons between sets use only the questions they share: the first 10 per file for ron,
the first 50 for en-cot.


Where the errors are
Key finding: 64% of wrong answers (19,583 / 30,421) are on three calendar tasks, and
Hindi CoT is barely better than Hindi direct (41.0% vs 45.3% wrong).

When the model reasons in English (English CoT, en-cot or ron), 78–88% of its wrong
answers are on day of week, next week and recurrences. The arithmetic tasks rarely go
wrong once it reasons. Direct answers get them wrong too, more so at higher difficulties.

Day of week
Key finding: direct answers on day of week are guesses (3,869 wrong answers, 12.7% of
all), and with reasoning most errors are exactly one day late.

Direct answers are random guesses: wrong 85% of the time (3,839 / 4,500), which is
chance level (85.7%), and spread evenly over the other six days. With English reasoning
the errors stop being random: more than half are exactly one day late.

responses/ · wrong day-of-week answers that name a weekday, all difficulties

     Why one day late: to count the days from Jan 1, the model adds up the month lengths
     plus the day of the month. That counts Jan 1 itself, so the total is one too high.
     Hindi CoT is still mostly guessing: wrong 79% of the time (1,778 / 2,250), with only a
     weak one-day-late peak. en-cot, on the same Hindi questions, gets 54% wrong (404 /
     750) and shows the clear peak.
     Dates further out are harder. English CoT is wrong 30% of the time on short (136 / 450,
     all 2025 dates) and 50% on very long (223 / 450, dates up to 2033).
     ron is wrong only 15% of the time (91 / 600), and many of those responses are cut off at
     16k tokens.

Example (English CoT, very_very_long)

     What day of the week is 2029-07-02? Correct: Monday. Model: Tuesday.

     "Jan 1, 2024 = Monday … 2029: Jan 1 to July 2 = 31+28+31+30+31+30+2 = 183 … 2010 ÷
     7 = 287 remainder 1. Monday + 1 = Tuesday."

Jan 1 to Jul 2 is 182 days. In another response the model makes the same slip twice, once in
its working and once in its check, and takes the matching results as proof it was right.

Example: the same slip in Hindi (Hindi CoT, short)

     2025-12-12 सप्ताह का कौन सा दिन है? Correct: शुक्रवार (Friday). Model: शनिवार (Saturday).

     "346 को 7 से भाग देने पर 49 पूर्ण सप्ताह और 3 दिन शेष … 1 जनवरी 2025, बुधवार था।" (346 ÷ 7 = 49
     weeks and 3 days … Jan 1, 2025 was a Wednesday.)

Jan 1 to Dec 12 is 345 days, not 346.

Recurrences
Key finding: recurrences stop one interval short in 1,698 wrong answers (5.6% of all).
Counting backwards is a smaller, Hindi-only pattern (396).

The question asks for the k-th occurrence after today, "without counting today". Two
mistakes explain most of the errors we can identify: stopping one interval early, and, on
Hindi questions, counting backwards from today.


   Condition                             Recurrence wrong      One interval short   Counted backwards

   standard, English, direct             80% (1,799 / 2,250)   30% (526 / 1,765)    1% (14 / 1,765)

   standard, English, CoT                17% (391 / 2,250)     21% (50 / 234)       3% (6 / 234)

   standard, Hindi, direct               97% (2,180 / 2,250)   19% (410 / 2,176)    7% (145 / 2,176)

   standard, Hindi, CoT                  79% (1,780 / 2,250)   35% (612 / 1,758)    9% (164 / 1,758)

   en-cot                                35% (259 / 750)       48% (99 / 207)       28% (58 / 207)


The last two columns are shares of wrong answers that give a date.

     One interval short is the same counting error as in day of week. The model counts
     today as the first occurrence, so it adds k−1 intervals instead of k.
     Counting backwards comes from reading Hindi. en-cot does it in 28% of its errors (58
     / 207) even though it reasons in English, against 3% for English CoT (6 / 234). It reads
     "आज को छोड़कर" (excluding today) as "the occurrence before today".
     The question wording is ungrammatical: "the 10 occurrence" in English, "1वीं आवृत्ति" and
     "हर 1 दिनों" in Hindi. This hurts most on short questions, where k is 1 or 2: en-cot gets 57%
     of short recurrences wrong (85 / 150) against 25% on medium (38 / 150).

Example: counting backwards (en-cot, short)

     आज 2025-04-10 है, और मेरे पास हर 4 दिनों पर एक पुनरावृत्ति है। आज को छोड़कर, 1वीं आवृत्ति की तिथि क्या
     है? Correct: 2025-04-14. Model: 2025-04-06.

     "Or going forward: 2025-04-14 (4 days after today, first occurrence after today).
     Given the phrasing 'excluding today,' this suggests we need to find what comes
     before."

The model finds the right date and then talks itself out of it because of how it reads the
Hindi phrase.

Example: one interval short, said outright (English CoT, very_long)


     Today is 2028-10-20, and I have a recurrence every 17 days. Without counting today,
     what is the date of the 9 occurrence? Correct: 2029-03-22. Model: 2029-03-05.

     "If first occurrence is 17 days after today: 9th occurrence = 8 × 17 = 136 days after
     today."


If the first occurrence is 17 days out, the 9th is 9 × 17 = 153 days out.

Example: counting backwards in Hindi (Hindi CoT, short)


     आज 2025-10-28 है, और मेरे पास हर 2 दिनों पर एक पुनरावृत्ति है। आज को छोड़कर, 1वीं आवृत्ति की तिथि क्या
     है? Correct: 2025-10-30. Model: 2025-10-26.

     "पुनरावृत्ति हर 2 दिनों पर होती है। इसका मतलब है कि पहली बार 2 दिन पहले हुआ था।" (It recurs every 2
     days, which means the first time was 2 days ago.)



Feb 29 and other boundaries
Key finding: Feb 29 in the span is behind 1,947 wrong answers (6.4% of all) and
multiplies the error rate in every condition, ron included.

If the span includes Feb 29, errors go up sharply in every condition, ron included.

Time arithmetic
Key finding: 3,760 wrong time answers (12.4% of all) have the minutes right and the
hours wrong.

In the time tasks the minutes are usually right and the hours are wrong: 41–65% of wrong
time answers are off by a whole number of hours.

Smaller pattern: Hindi CoT subtracts by walking forward
Covers 212 wrong answers directly (0.7% of all), but explains why Hindi CoT does worse
than Hindi direct on subtraction.

On date subtraction, Hindi CoT gets worse much faster than any other condition. The
reason is one habit: it subtracts using the method for adding. It counts forward to the end
of the current month, then steps through the next month instead of the previous one.

Hindi, not from reading it. All five very_very_long errors we sampled used this method.

What the parsed reasoning shows
Aditya's parser ( parser.py ) turns each reasoning trace into a sequence of labelled steps:
 PLAN , ADD , SUB , LEAP , CHECK , REVISE ("wait, actually…"), IGNORE (talking about the
distractor), FORMAT (arguing about output format or language), and so on. We parsed
every response that has reasoning: standard CoT, en-cot, and ron's hidden reasoning,
55,167 traces in all. The parser labels 96–97% of sentences in standard CoT and en-cot,
and 82–89% in ron.

Key finding: format deliberation fills a third of ron's Hindi direct reasoning (207,097 of
627,487 steps), but it doesn't make answers wrong.




parsed_traces.csv · 55,167 parsed traces (standard CoT, en-cot, ron hidden reasoning) · steps labelled by

     It comes from the prompt's rules. The Hindi direct prompt asks for "only the final
     answer", the whole response in Hindi, the word "Answer:" in English and Western digits,
     all at once. ron's hidden reasoning argues about these instead of the arithmetic. Visible
     CoT almost never does (0.2–0.7% of steps).
     It's worst where the maths is easiest: 39% of ron's steps on date addition and 34–38%
     on the time tasks, against 9–10% on day of week and next week. It falls from 27% of
     steps on short to 15% on very_very_long.
     It doesn't predict errors. Right ron Hindi direct traces have more FORMAT steps than
     wrong ones (157 vs 118 on average). It wastes tokens rather than changing answers.

Key finding: long re-checking loops mark wrong and cut-off answers.

     On every task, English CoT's wrong traces have 1.5–6 times more CHECK and REVISE
     steps than its right ones. For example, time durations average 12.4 against 2.0, and
     recurrences 19.3 against 5.0.
     Cut-off English CoT traces average 42 re-checking steps against 8 for wrong traces
     that did finish. 869 of English CoT's 2,903 wrong traces (30%) have 20 or more.

Key finding: Hindi CoT almost never checks its work. Only 744 of 20,250 Hindi CoT traces
(3.7%) contain a CHECK or REVISE step, against 17,820 / 20,250 (88%) for English CoT and
5,132 / 6,750 (76%) for en-cot. Hindi CoT traces are also much shorter (median 13 steps
against 25 for English CoT). This fits it being barely better than answering directly.

     Feb 29 errors aren't from forgetting leap years. 185 of 190 wrong English CoT answers
     on Feb 29 questions mention the leap year ( LEAP ), as do 78 / 83 for en-cot and 416 /
     508 for Hindi CoT. The model knows it's a leap year and still counts wrong.

Caveats
     ron and en-cot are small: once split by task and difficulty, their cells hold 30–150
     responses, so ignore differences of a few points there.
     The labels come from a script. "Other" is still 66% of standard errors, so the labels
     don't explain every error. The examples were checked by hand; the percentages
     weren't.
     Cut-off responses count as wrong, which matters most for ron.
     Scoring uses metrics.py on main . Rahul's newer parser fix may shift the Hindi
     numbers slightly.
